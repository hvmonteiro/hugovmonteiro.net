Attending my first startup summit helped me see that my experience can be useful in this ecosystem. The conversations and ideas made me realize that I can bring a practical view shaped by many years of building products, designing systems, and working in corporate R&D.

My plan is to take part in startup communities and ecosystems in Lisbon, Portugal, and anywhere else where my experience may be useful. I want to learn from founders, understand their real challenges, and find the best way to contribute.

I have been developing tools and applications for my own use for as long as I can remember. For many years, I did not consider that some of them could have market value. After seeing small open-source projects grow into large companies, I started to look at my own ideas differently. I would like to explore whether some of those ideas can also become useful products and, perhaps, successful businesses.

I also want to be involved in the startup ecosystem because I feel a strong need to help others. My career path is uncommon, and it has given me knowledge across technology, business, R&D, architecture, delivery, and people. I want to use that experience to help founders and young professionals avoid problems, make better decisions, and build with a longer-term view.

## The experience I can bring

Many corporate R&D teams I have worked with operated in a startup-like way: small teams, new products, uncertain requirements, limited resources, and the need to make decisions quickly. Corporate environments can sometimes move more slowly, but many of the technical and team challenges are similar.

My main strength comes from working as an architect and solution designer. I know how to build for the long term, plan for scalability from the first day, connect different systems, and avoid technical decisions that become expensive later. Early-stage teams often do not have this experience yet because their first priority is proving that the idea works. This is where I believe I can add value.

## Areas I am preparing to support

- **The problem and the first product.** Understand the real need, test the main ideas, and define a sensible first version.
- **Technology choices.** Discuss architecture, integration, security, build-versus-buy decisions, and the cost of each option.
- **Scalability from the start.** Build a foundation that can grow without creating unnecessary complexity too early.
- **Delivery and team habits.** Use clear and practical ways of working so progress and problems remain visible.
- **Long-term thinking.** Balance the need to move quickly with decisions that will still make sense later.

## Speaking and sharing

I am also preparing to become a speaker at summits and conferences. I want to speak about bleeding-edge technology, AI, cybersecurity, R&D software development, architecture, mentoring, and the lessons I have learned throughout my career. Speaking will allow me to share practical knowledge, meet people across these communities, and build a public profile based on real experience.

For now, this page describes the direction I am preparing. As I become involved in startup communities and gain direct mentoring experience, I will update it with real work and lessons learned.
